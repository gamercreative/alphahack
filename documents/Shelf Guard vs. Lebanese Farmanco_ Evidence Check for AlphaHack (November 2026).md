# Shelf Guard vs. Lebanese Farmanco: Evidence Check for AlphaHack (as of 14 Nov 2026)

Shelf Guard targets a real problem that nobody has solved: Lebanese pharmacies still carry expired and near-expiry stock that importers only partly take back.\[1\] That said, two of its pitch claims need fixing before you lock the idea. First, the 29 Oct 2026 "ban" covers biosimilar (biological "similar") medicines, not all generics, and no product list has been published. Second, the importers' "4 packages" rule is an unwritten protocol, and it may have been changed by a new OPL–importers protocol signed on 23 Sept 2026.

## TL;DR
- **Build Shelf Guard, but lead with expiry and returns, not the ban.** The returns dispute is well documented. It runs from the 1 June 2022 MoPH decision, through the State Council annulment of 25 Jan 2024, the "4 packages" protocol and the rejected 5+2 proposal, to a new protocol signed 23 Sept 2026 whose terms are unpublished. OPL President Merkabawi publicly blames delayed returns for pharmacy closures.
- **The 29 Oct 2026 measure concerns biosimilars from "non-reference" countries.** That is a narrow, mostly specialty and hospital category, and no MoPH list of affected products, batches or registration numbers has been published. Don't build your demo on a list that doesn't exist. Use MoPH's regularly published recall decisions instead, which you can verify.
- **Lebanese Farmanco is the weaker option for a 48-hour hackathon.** Lebanon has no public pharmacist shortage-reporting system, so the gap is real. But the Dutch model depends on supplier verification. Lebanon's importers' association calls itself the only body authorised to report stock levels, and OPL itself said in Sept 2026 that medicines are currently available. You could not show 20 real verified cases in 48 hours.

## Details: What the Evidence Means for Your Build

**The returns problem is real, recurring and political.** The law (Art. 53) gives pharmacists an uncapped right to return near-expiry stock. In practice importers apply a protocol of about 4 packages per item, with a reporting window of about 3 months before expiry.\[3\] Each side accuses the other: LPIA says some pharmacies are trying to return stock that expired during the shortage years.\[12\] This is a good setting for an agent. Each week, someone in the pharmacy has to sweep the shelves for items entering the 3-month window, group them by importer, fill in each importer's return list, respect the per-item cap, and decide what to return, discount, or move to another pharmacy. Cases that exceed the cap are natural human-in-the-loop decisions, and undoing a mistaken return line is a natural "real undo".

**The ban hook is weaker than it looks.** The suspension applies to biosimilars, which are biologics such as oncology and immunology drugs and insulins, often dispensed through hospitals or MoPH channels. It does not cover ordinary community-pharmacy generics. No list of affected products or batches has been published. Instead of "Oct 29 ban compliance", build a **recall-compliance module** fed by MoPH's published "Pharmaceutical Products Recalled from the Lebanese Market" decisions. 2026 alone has many of these: Decisions 2/1, 392/1, 457/1, 619/1, 1250/1, 1525/1, 1539/1, 1784/1, 1815/1 and 2013–2019/1 (13 Oct 2026).\[47\]\[48\] This is a real public data source, and matching batch numbers from a DataMatrix scan against it is a concrete, demo-able task.

**Barcodes make the "20 real cases" feasible.** Every legally imported pack since 2019, and every local pack since 2023, carries a GS1 DataMatrix with GTIN, batch and expiry.\[23\]\[49\] A phone scan gives the agent its structured input without needing MediTrack access.

**Why Farmanco is weaker.** The Dutch model depends on suppliers confirming shortages.\[42\] Lebanon's importer syndicate explicitly says it alone may declare stock levels, and it has warned against "incorrect numbers" that cause panic.\[46\] OPL's own head said in Sept 2026 that there is no black market now because medicines are available.\[8\] A shortage board needs a network of many pharmacies to be useful and cannot produce verified cases in 48 hours.

## Recommendations
1. **Lock Shelf Guard, reframed as "expiry and returns agent with recall checks".** Core loop: scan the DataMatrix, flag items within 3 months of expiry, draft a return list per importer respecting the ~4-per-item protocol, flag recalled batches, then the pharmacist approves or undoes each line, and the cost of each run is logged.
2. **Top 5 verified pitch facts:** (a) Merkabawi, 9 Sept 2026: 200 pharmacies closed and 700 threatened.\[38\] (b) Merkabawi, Sept 2026: importers' delayed returns are a cause of the crisis, and pharmacists absorb expired stock (The Beiruter, Houna Lebanon).\[8\]\[37\] (c) The State Council annulled the 2022 returns ban on 25 Jan 2024, yet OPL says compliance is still incomplete.\[1\] (d) OPL: pharmacists are "not legally authorised to destroy medicines" because no official infrastructure exists.\[1\] (e) The World Bank says MoPH should map where expired medicines are stored, which means nobody knows how much exists.\[10\]
3. **Get your "real voice" from a pharmacist outside Beirut.** Merkabawi says pressure is highest in suburbs and peripheral areas.\[8\] Ask them to show you a real return list and how many packages the importer actually accepted.
4. **Before the pitch, ask OPL for the 23 Sept 2026 protocol terms** so you don't present an outdated cap.

## Caveats
- The terms of the 23 Sept 2026 protocol are unpublished. The "4 packages" and "3-month notice" rules come from 2025 reporting and may have changed.
- The date of Al-Akhbar's 5+2 article, the ~22%/1–1.5% margin figures, the WHO 5,000 readers / 1,000 pharmacies figures, the "~280 drugs" figure, and the expiry features of Lebanese pharmacy software were **not verified**.
- Pharmacy-distress figures come from the OPL president's interviews and vary between outlets (600 vs 700 at risk; 3,600 vs 3,700 pharmacies).\[37\]\[38\] This is advocacy data, not an audited census.
- English coverage that calls the 29 Oct measure a ban on "generic drugs" (L'Orient Today) conflicts with Arabic and official wording ("similar biological drugs").\[13\]\[16\] Treat the measure as limited to biosimilars unless MoPH publishes otherwise.
