1 · Problem statement

A scarce medicine means a weekly hunt

In Lebanon, the patient calls or walks to pharmacy after pharmacy. Pharmacists phone each other. Nobody knows who has stock, at what price, or whether the box is genuine. People who can afford it buy in bulk, which makes the shortage worse. A gap in supply is medically dangerous.

Who it is for

  • Patients and caregivers who depend on a recurring scarce medicine: one request or one schedule instead of hours of calls and chats every week.
  • Pharmacists who search for a medicine for their own customer: one enquiry reaches several pharmacies, with undo and follow-up.
  • Pharmacies receive clear, structured asks and never see the client's identity.

The job it solves

Find the medicine the client asked for, at a pharmacy that really has it, with a verified box, the best price and the latest expiry, without the client chasing anyone and without an AI making a medical decision.

Before

  • Call pharmacy after pharmacy
  • Haggle over strength and price
  • Hope the box is real
  • Buy in bulk "just in case"

With DOSYN

  • Ask once, or every Friday
  • Wassit asks several pharmacies at once
  • Rules check matches, doses and photos
  • Best verified offer, then reserve

What Wassit does, step by step

Code decides what is allowed. The model only decides what to do next. gold = a person approves

1

Client asks

Searches the catalogue; asks once or on a schedule (e.g. every Friday). No bulk buying.

Client
2

Match the medicine

Wassit searches the DB catalogue. Ambiguous request → it asks the requester.

Requester answers clarifications
3

Pick pharmacies

Go ranks candidates (stock, distance, freshness); Wassit proposes targets.

Requester approves targets. Client tickets: Go auto-approves only if every target is in the ranked list
4

Ask anonymously

Outbox sends to several pharmacies at once as "DOSYN client desk".

60 s undo window on every outbound message
5

Pharmacists reply

Free text: Arabic, Arabizi, French, English. Silence → one follow-up → no_response, never "unavailable".

Only target pharmacy staff can reply or upload photos
6

Read the reply

Wassit turns it into a structured offer with the exact quoted words as evidence.

Wassit · relayed
7

Rules decide

Go decides match and dose equivalence: same ingredient + form + listed in the government reference. 625mg can never make 1000mg.

Rules
8

Check the photo

Pharmacist types code + expiry; vision model reads the photo; Go compares.

Flagged photos go to a human reviewer
9

Best offer

Go picks from verified offers only: lowest total, then latest expiry. The model never ranks.

Rules
10

Reserve

The client sees the best verified offer in the ticket and reserves it.

The client reserves

5 · Business model

Who pays, how much, and why

Status: proposed, not validated. The owner's brief lists this as an open question: "subscription for patients and caregivers, or B2B with insurers, NGOs and chronic-care clinics". The proposal below follows that brief; no price has been tested with customers.

1 · Patients and caregivers

Who: people on a recurring scarce medicine.

What: one-off requests free; a subscription for scheduled medicines (e.g. every Friday).

Why: hours of calls every cycle become one schedule and a pickup notice.

2 · B2B: insurers, NGOs, chronic-care clinics

Who: organisations responsible for patients on chronic medicine.

What: per-patient monthly fee for supply monitoring.

Why: a supply gap is medically dangerous and costly; they get status and history in one place.

3 · Pharmacies

Who: participating pharmacies.

What: free during a pilot, since adoption is unproven; later an optional per-fulfilled-ticket fee.

Why: clear structured asks and reservations they would otherwise miss.

Why the margin can work (measured)

Running cost per enquiry is low: the qwen 20-case run cost $0 on our own GPU; the same 20 cases on claude-haiku-5-5 cost $0.108 in total (2026-11-14). Every agent run's tokens and cost are stored in Go.

Why it compounds (owner's brief)

The hunt repeats every cycle. Each conversation becomes structured data: who answers fast, whose stock claims hold. That pharmacy-reliability map is a roadmap item, not built yet.

6 · Anything else

What's next, limits, lessons learned

What's next

  • Replace placeholder cases with the owner's 20 real cases
  • WhatsApp Business adapter (same engine)
  • Voice notes from pharmacists, with human confirmation on any number
  • Pilot: one city, 10 to 20 patients, a handful of partner pharmacies
  • More recurring scarce medicines: insulin, thyroid, epilepsy
  • Real MoPH reference data and regulatory sign-off on dose rules

Limits, stated plainly

  • The local model is slow: 1 to 2.5 min per enquiry, about 64k input tokens
  • Government reference rows are demo seed, not real MoPH data
  • Dose equivalence needs regulatory sign-off
  • Photo reading was tested on a clean synthetic image
  • Pharmacy adoption is unproven
  • The 20 cases are placeholders

Lessons learned

  • Put guards in code, not in the prompt. Three guards (word-by-word search retry, end the run after wait/close/ask, backend refuses premature closes) moved qwen to 16/20.
  • Let the model choose and the tools compute. Ranking, dose maths and closing are Go.
  • Case design matters: 3 of qwen's 4 misses come from how placeholder cases are written.
  • Pick the model per job: qwen fails on images, gemma4:31b reads the test box.
